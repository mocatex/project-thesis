\section{Experimental Setup}

All experiments were conducted on a 10\% subset of the CC-News corpus spanning 2016–2021. Media sentiment and topic trends were aggregated at the yearly level unless stated otherwise. The system automatically generated analytical plans, selected NLP tools, executed the analyses, and produced visualizations for each question. Selected figures are shown to support the qualitative interpretation of results.

The analytical pipeline relies on a set of modular NLP tools that are invoked dynamically by the LLM during execution. For the purpose of this thesis, the tools were implemented with mocked outputs to demonstrate the system architecture, planning behavior, and result integration without relying on fully operational external services. Each tool represents a distinct analytical capability commonly used in large-scale text analysis.

\subsection{NLP Analysis Tools}

The following NLP tools were available to the system during execution. All tools were implemented with mocked outputs for demonstration and evaluation purposes:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{sentiment\_over\_time}: Computes aggregated sentiment trends across time intervals.
    \item \texttt{volatility\_of\_sentiment}: Measures temporal fluctuations in sentiment intensity.
    \item \texttt{emotion\_distribution\_over\_time}: Tracks the distribution of emotional categories over time.
    \item \texttt{topic\_trend\_over\_time}: Identifies and tracks dominant topics across temporal segments.
    \item \texttt{dynamic\_topic\_shift\_detection}: Detects significant changes in topic prevalence.
    \item \texttt{event\_detection}: Identifies temporally localized events reflected in the corpus.
    \item \texttt{burst\_detection}: Detects sudden increases in coverage or term usage.
    \item \texttt{named\_entity\_trend\_tracking}: Tracks the temporal prominence of named entities.
    \item \texttt{relationship\_graph\_extraction}: Extracts co-occurrence graphs between entities.
    \item \texttt{framing\_shift\_detection}: Identifies changes in narrative framing over time.
    \item \texttt{stance\_detection\_over\_time}: Tracks changes in expressed stance toward entities or topics.
    \item \texttt{salient\_quote\_extraction}: Extracts representative or influential quotations.
    \item \texttt{fact\_density\_estimation}: Estimates the ratio of factual statements within documents.
    \item \texttt{source\_bias\_comparison}: Compares sentiment and framing across sources.
    \item \texttt{temporal\_segmentation}: Segments the corpus into meaningful temporal phases.
    \item \texttt{document\_clustering}: Groups documents based on semantic similarity.
    \item \texttt{keyphrase\_evolution\_tracking}: Tracks the emergence and decline of keyphrases.
    \item \texttt{anomaly\_etection\_in\_discourse}: Identifies atypical discourse patterns.
    \item \texttt{confidence\_scoring\_of\_results}: Estimates confidence scores for aggregated findings.
\end{itemize}

\textit{"How did media sentiment towards Donald Trump evolve from the 2016 campaign through his presidency, and how did these sentiment patterns relate to periods of heightened US equity-market volatility?"}

\begin{quote}

Measured yearly average sentiment in the supplied analytics: 2016 (strongly negative), 2017 (negative), 2018 (an outlier positive mean in the series), 2019 (strongly negative), 2020 (negative). Sentiment volatility was high (avg change ~0.75) and the analytics flag strong bursts in 2017–2020.

The qualitative reporting links those sentiment shifts to equity-market volatility in a patterned way: sometimes media negativity coincided with elevated market fear (2018 tariffs, 2019 trade-tweet episodes, 2020 COVID sell-off and election jitters), but other periods showed a disconnect (2017: negative political coverage while major indices pushed to new highs and the VIX stayed low).

\paragraph{Analytical Results}

The following results describe the outputs produced by the proposed system when applied to this question, illustrating how the framework translates large-scale media analytics into a coherent temporal narrative.

Sentiment was strongly negative in 2016 and 2017, shifted to a positive annual mean in 2018, and returned to strongly negative levels in 2019 before moderating slightly in 2020. Across all years, sentiment volatility was high and burst detection identified pronounced spikes in coverage between 2017 and 2020.

\paragraph{Relationship to Market Volatility}

Periods of heightened negative or uncertain media coverage sometimes coincided with elevated equity-market volatility. Notable alignments appear during the 2018 trade-policy escalations, repeated trade-related announcements in 2019, and the COVID-19 market shock in 2020. In these periods, coverage explicitly framed political decisions as market-relevant risk factors.

However, the relationship was not uniform. In 2017, negative political coverage coexisted with relatively subdued realized market volatility, suggesting a disconnect between political sentiment and immediate equity-market reactions. This indicates that negative media sentiment does not consistently translate into realized volatility and that market responses depend on the perceived economic relevance of events.

\paragraph{Analytical Results}

Media coverage of Facebook exhibited a clear temporal shift in dominant framing between 2016 and 2019. In 2019, continued regulatory scrutiny and legal settlements were linked to additional sharp, short-term market-value declines, followed by only limited positive reactions to regulatory resolution.

Taken together, the results suggest that the transition from innovation-focused to regulation-dominated media coverage corresponded with a re-evaluation of Facebook’s risk profile by financial markets. While innovation narratives had previously supported optimistic valuation expectations, sustained regulatory framing introduced long-term uncertainty that was reflected in market reactions.

\subsection{Media Framing of COVID-19 from Outbreak to Vaccine Rollout}

\paragraph{User Query}

\textit{"How did media tone and dominant framing around COVID-19 evolve from the initial outbreak through vaccine rollout (late 2019–2021), and how did public-health, economic, and civil-liberty frames compete over time?"}

\paragraph{System-Generated Summary}
The system produced the following condensed answer based on the retrieved corpus and analytical outputs:

\begin{quote}
Media coverage of COVID-19 evolved from an initial public-health crisis framing in late 2019 and early 2020 toward a strongly innovation- and solution-oriented narrative during 2020, dominated by vaccine research and development. Despite the ongoing pandemic, overall sentiment in 2020 was unusually positive in the aggregated analytics. During 2021, sentiment declined and coverage became more conflicted, with increasing emphasis on regulation, civil-liberty debates, and economic reopening alongside continued public-health concerns.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Analytical Results}

Media sentiment and framing around COVID-19 exhibited distinct temporal phases between late 2019 and 2021. Early coverage during the outbreak period was characterized by an urgent risk and crisis tone, focusing on epidemiology, containment measures, and immediate public-health threats. As the pandemic progressed through 2020, aggregated sentiment shifted markedly toward positive values, coinciding with increased reporting on vaccine research, clinical trials, and technological solutions.

In 2021, sentiment declined and became net negative in the annual aggregates. Coverage during this phase placed greater emphasis on the complexities of vaccine rollout, including safety concerns, supply constraints, regulatory approval processes, and public resistance. While technological and medical innovation remained salient, the overall tone reflected growing societal tension and uncertainty.

\paragraph{Competing Frames Over Time}

Framing analysis reveals a clear shift in the relative prominence of public-health, economic, and civil-liberty narratives. During the initial outbreak phase, public-health risk dominated the media agenda, with economic and civil-liberty considerations appearing as secondary concerns. In 2020, innovation and growth-oriented framing—particularly around vaccine development—became the dominant narrative at the corpus level, reducing the relative weight of pure crisis framing in annual aggregates.

By 2021, economic and civil-liberty frames rose substantially and began to directly compete with public-health narratives. Coverage increasingly focused on reopening strategies, labor and market impacts, and legal or ethical debates surrounding mandates and restrictions. This shift is reflected in rising topic weights for economy-related themes and increased prominence of regulatory and civil-liberty-related keyphrases during the rollout period.

\paragraph{Analytical Workflow}

To address this question, the system autonomously segmented the corpus into outbreak, pandemic, and vaccine-rollout phases and selected sentiment analysis, topic trend analysis, framing-shift detection, and keyphrase evolution tracking tools. These analyses were combined to identify changes in overall tone, dominant themes, and competing narrative frames across time, enabling a comparative longitudinal interpretation of media coverage.

\paragraph{Summary}

In summary, media coverage of COVID-19 transitioned from an initial health-crisis framing to a predominantly innovation-focused narrative during 2020, before becoming more contested and polarized during the vaccine rollout in 2021. While public-health concerns remained central throughout, economic and civil-liberty frames gained increasing prominence over time, resulting in a more conflicted media narrative during the later stages of the pandemic.


\subsection{Media Coverage of Greta Thunberg and Youth Climate Activism}

\paragraph{User Query}

\textit{"How did media coverage of Greta Thunberg and youth climate activism evolve from 2018 to 2021, and how did framing and tone vary across regions and outlet types?"}

\paragraph{System-Generated Summary}

The system produced the following condensed answer based on the retrieved corpus and analytical outputs:

\begin{quote}
Media coverage of Greta Thunberg rose sharply in 2019, then declined in volume but remained visible through 2021 while its emphasis shifted. The year 2019 marked a tipping point characterized by intense personalization, strong policy reporting, and a polarized backlash. Coverage in 2020 shifted toward adaptive and tactical narratives, while 2021 increasingly framed Thunberg as a celebrity-activist linked to formal policy moments, with a more balanced and sympathetic tone than in 2019.

Framing varied systematically by region and outlet type: European and international mainstream outlets tended toward sympathetic personalization combined with policy amplification, whereas conservative and right-wing outlets emphasized skepticism and backlash.

\paragraph{Analytical Results}

Entity-tracking analysis indicates that media attention toward Greta Thunberg was minimal prior to 2019, followed by a pronounced spike in coverage during that year. After this peak, coverage volume declined but remained consistently present through 2020 and 2021, indicating sustained media relevance beyond the initial surge.

Framing analysis reveals a clear temporal evolution. Coverage prior to 2019 was sparse and primarily framed through regulatory or ethical lenses. In 2019, reporting combined strong personalization with policy-oriented narratives surrounding climate action, accompanied by heightened polarization. During 2020, the dominant framing shifted toward innovation and adaptation, reflecting reporting on digital activism and new mobilization strategies during the pandemic. By 2021, coverage increasingly situated Thunberg within formal policy contexts, such as international climate summits, while maintaining a level of personalization.

\paragraph{Polarization and Stance Dynamics}

Automated stance detection shows that 2019 exhibited the highest degree of polarization, with a substantial proportion of articles classified as oppositional. In subsequent years, the relative share of supportive and neutral coverage increased, while explicit opposition declined. This suggests that the peak of media backlash occurred around Thunberg’s initial rise to prominence, followed by a normalization of coverage as her role became institutionalized within climate-policy discourse.

\paragraph{Regional and Outlet-Type Differences}

The analysis identifies systematic differences across media sources. European and international mainstream outlets were more likely to combine sympathetic personalization with policy-focused reporting, particularly in later years. Activist, local, and regional outlets emphasized movement-building, solidarity, and collective action. In contrast, conservative and right-wing outlets displayed higher levels of skepticism and critical framing, contributing disproportionately to the polarization observed in 2019. Wire services and centrist outlets tended to adopt more neutral, policy-balanced reporting styles.

\paragraph{Analytical Workflow}

To address this question, the system selected tools for named-entity trend tracking, framing-shift detection, stance detection over time, topic trend analysis, and source comparison. These tools were combined to assess changes in visibility, dominant narratives, and sentiment across years and outlet types, enabling a comparative longitudinal analysis of media treatment of youth climate activism.

\paragraph{Summary}

In summary, media coverage of Greta Thunberg evolved from minimal attention to intense, polarized visibility in 2019, followed by a period of sustained but more institutionalized reporting through 2021. While early coverage combined personalization with policy urgency and backlash, later reporting increasingly emphasized formal political engagement and climate governance. Regional and outlet-type differences played a significant role in shaping tone and framing, demonstrating the system’s ability to surface heterogeneity in media narratives across sources.

\section{Hard / Multi-Hop Questions}

This section evaluates the system’s ability to resolve indirect, multi-hop entity references before performing corpus analysis. Unlike the core longitudinal questions, this task requires external knowledge resolution prior to document retrieval.

\subsection{Multi-Hop Entity Resolution: Melinda French Gates and the 2021 Divorce}


\paragraph{User Query}

\textit{"How did media coverage of the spouse of the Microsoft co-founder who runs a major philanthropic foundation change around the 2021 divorce announcement?"}

\paragraph{System-Generated Summary}

The system produced the following condensed answer based on the retrieved corpus and analytical outputs:

\begin{quote}
Around the May 2021 divorce announcement, coverage of Melinda French Gates shifted from her long-standing public identity as a philanthropic leader to a focus on the mechanics and institutional fallout of the split. Reporting emphasized legal and financial arrangements, asset transfers, and governance implications for the Bill \& Melinda Gates Foundation, accompanied by increased attention to ethics and institutional accountability.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Multi-Hop Reasoning and Entity Resolution}

To answer this question, the system first resolved the indirect description \textit{"the spouse of the Microsoft co-founder who runs a major philanthropic foundation"} via an external knowledge lookup. This process identified the target entity as \textbf{Melinda French Gates}, linking her to Bill Gates and the Bill \& Melinda Gates Foundation. Only after this entity resolution step did the system proceed with corpus retrieval and analysis.

This demonstrates the system’s ability to perform multi-hop reasoning by combining external knowledge resolution with internal document analysis, a capability not required for simpler, entity-explicit queries.

\paragraph{Analytical Results}

Qualitative summaries and aggregated analytics indicate a clear narrative shift in 2021. Prior to the divorce announcement, Melinda French Gates was primarily portrayed as a philanthropic leader and institutional partner within the Foundation. Following the announcement, coverage pivoted toward divorce logistics, legal and financial arrangements, and questions surrounding governance and control of the Foundation.

Quantitatively, the analytics for 2021 show a slightly negative average sentiment (avg\_sentiment $\approx$ -0.08, $n=100$ documents). Framing analysis indicates that ethics and regulation-oriented narratives dominated coverage (approximately 47\%), followed by innovation and growth-related frames (approximately 36\%), with risk and crisis framing playing a smaller role. This framing distribution aligns with reporting that emphasized institutional accountability and governance implications rather than personal or philanthropic achievements.

\paragraph{Entity Attention and Coverage Focus}

Named-entity tracking reveals that coverage remained disproportionately focused on Bill Gates, with higher mention counts for ``Gates'' and ``Bill'' than for ``Melinda''. This suggests that even when analyzing Melinda French Gates as the primary subject, media narratives continued to frame the divorce predominantly through Bill Gates’s public persona, with Melinda’s role contextualized in relation to institutional and legal consequences.

\paragraph{Limitations of the Analysis}

The provided analytics represent an aggregated snapshot of coverage in 2021 and do not include a fine-grained pre- and post-announcement time series. As a result, the system cannot quantitatively measure the magnitude or immediacy of coverage changes around the May 2021 announcement date, nor detect abrupt topic shifts using dynamic change-point analysis.

\paragraph{Summary}

In summary, the system successfully resolved an indirect, multi-hop entity reference and identified a clear shift in media coverage following the 2021 divorce announcement. Coverage reoriented from Melinda French Gates’s philanthropic identity toward legal, financial, and governance-related narratives, framed primarily through ethics and institutional accountability. This case demonstrates the system’s capability to integrate external knowledge resolution with corpus-level analysis while also transparently reporting analytical limitations.

\section{Negative-Control Questions: No real Relationship Expected}

This subsection reports a negative-control experiment designed to evaluate whether the system avoids constructing spurious relationships when no meaningful link is expected.

\subsection{Negative Control: Bitcoin Price Movements vs. Colgate-Palmolive Media Sentiment (2016--2021)}


\paragraph{User Query}

\textit{"Did Bitcoin price movements (2016--2021) have any systematic impact on media sentiment towards Colgate-Palmolive?"}

\paragraph{System-Generated Summary}

The system produced the following condensed answer based on the retrieved corpus and analytical outputs:

\begin{quote}
Based on the provided corpus and analytics, there is no evidence that Bitcoin price movements (2016--2021) had a systematic impact on media sentiment toward Colgate-Palmolive. Across the yearly summaries and sentiment signals, Colgate coverage is driven by company- and sector-specific fundamentals, and Bitcoin/crypto is not invoked as an explanatory factor.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Analytical Results}

Across the retrieved Colgate-Palmolive documents and year-level summaries, the system did not identify recurring references to Bitcoin or crypto markets as drivers of Colgate-related media sentiment. Instead, the dominant narrative in the retrieved coverage is consistently grounded in firm and sector fundamentals, including earnings-related reporting, analyst ratings, and institutional or market positioning.

Year-level summarization did not surface evidence of systematic co-movement between crypto-driven news cycles and Colgate sentiment. While the sentiment-over-time outputs show variation across years and burst detection indicates years with increased Colgate coverage volume, the retrieved summaries and entity-level trends do not connect these bursts to Bitcoin events or crypto price narratives.

\paragraph{System Behavior on a ``No-Link Expected'' Query}

From a system perspective, this negative control demonstrates two properties of the framework. First, the pipeline preserves grounding by restricting synthesis to signals present in retrieved documents and derived analytics. Second, when the evidence does not support a relationship, the system produces a conservative conclusion rather than inferring a causal narrative. This behavior is essential for large-corpus exploration tasks, where coincidental temporal patterns can otherwise lead to misleading interpretations.

\paragraph{Limitations and Requirements for Causal Testing}

Although the system did not find evidence for a systematic impact, this does not constitute a definitive causal proof of ``no relationship''. The analysis is limited to the internal corpus and the available derived signals. A rigorous causal evaluation would require time-aligned Bitcoin price series, finer-grained timestamps for articles, and formal statistical causal tests. In particular, a robust test would include (i) event-window analysis around Bitcoin volatility episodes, (ii) article-level indicators for whether Colgate articles explicitly reference crypto, and (iii) time-series methods such as Granger causality or related lead--lag tests with controls for confounding macroeconomic or sector shocks.

\paragraph{Summary}

In summary, the negative-control experiment yields no evidence of a systematic relationship between Bitcoin price movements and media sentiment toward Colgate-Palmolive within the provided corpus and analytics. Importantly, the system responded by explicitly reporting insufficient evidence for a link and by specifying what additional data and methods would be required to test causality more rigorously. This outcome supports the system's reliability in avoiding spurious correlations during exploratory analysis.

\section{Counterfactual Controls: Plausible but Non-Occurring Events}

This section evaluates the system’s ability to detect and correctly handle questions that are plausible in form but rely on assumptions about events that did not occur in the real world. Such counterfactual controls are important to assess whether the system avoids producing analyses based on false premises and instead responds conservatively and transparently.

\subsection{Amazon and a Hypothetical AWS IPO (2019)}

\paragraph{User Query}

\textit{"How did media coverage of Amazon change after it spun off AWS via a separate IPO in 2019, particularly regarding antitrust and valuation framing (2018--2020)?"}

\paragraph{System Response}

The system determined that the question could not be answered as posed and produced the following response:

\begin{quote}
I cannot reliably answer this question because it is based on a premise that does not correspond to real-world events. The question assumes AWS was spun off via a separate IPO in 2019. That did not occur in the real world---AWS remained part of Amazon throughout 2016--2021---so the corpus (real-world news from 2016 to 2021) contains no coverage of such an IPO. Therefore, the question as posed is not answerable from the corpus.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Interpretation}

This outcome illustrates a key safeguard in the pipeline. Rather than attempting to infer or hallucinate media effects for an event that never occurred, the system explicitly rejected the premise of the question. The rejection was not based on temporal mismatch, but on factual inconsistency between the assumed event and real-world news coverage represented in the corpus.

Importantly, the system distinguished between related but valid topics---such as Amazon’s antitrust scrutiny, valuation debates, and the strategic role of AWS during 2018--2020---and the specific assumed event (a separate AWS IPO). While the former are present in the corpus, the latter is not, and therefore cannot serve as a basis for causal or comparative analysis.

\paragraph{System Behavior and Robustness}

From a system-design perspective, this counterfactual control demonstrates that the pipeline performs premise validation before retrieval and synthesis. This prevents misleading analyses that might otherwise arise from superficially coherent but incorrect user questions. Such behavior is particularly important in exploratory settings, where users may pose speculative or hypothetically framed questions that resemble real events.

\paragraph{Alternative Analyses}

When rejecting the original query, the system indicated viable alternative analytical paths that remain grounded in real data. In particular, it could (i) analyze actual media coverage of Amazon and AWS separately during 2018--2020, including antitrust and valuation framing, or (ii) explicitly frame a response as a hypothetical or counterfactual discussion rather than an empirical result. Both alternatives preserve methodological transparency while avoiding unsupported conclusions.

\paragraph{Summary}

In summary, this counterfactual control confirms that the system correctly identifies and rejects questions based on non-occurring events. Rather than producing speculative results, it reports the limitation clearly and suggests grounded alternatives. This behavior supports the reliability of the system for large-scale corpus exploration and highlights the importance of premise validation as a core component of LLM-assisted analytical pipelines.
